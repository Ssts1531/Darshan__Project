    Ugadi

    Ugadi marks the Hindu lunar new year in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana. The defining speciality of the festival is Ugadi Pachadi, a unique chutney-like dish made with six ingredients (neem, jaggery, tamarind, raw mango, salt, and chili) representing the six flavors—and emotional realities—of life.

    Symbolism: The mixture of sweet (joy), bitter (sorrow), sour (challenges), tangy (surprises), salty (fear), and spicy (anger) reminds people to accept all of life's experiences with an even mind.

    Festive Foods: Along with Pachadi, homes feature special dishes like Bobbatlu, Pulihora, and Vada.

    Decorations: Doors are draped with Torana (mango leaves and flowers) to invite prosperity, and colorful rangoli designs are drawn at entrances.

    Panchanga Shravanam: A key ritual is listening to the reading of the Panchanga (astronomical almanac) to know the astrological predictions for the upcoming year.

    The Karthigai Deepam Festival

    The Karthigai Deepam festival at the Arulmigu Arunachaleswarar Temple in Thiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu.

    This festival is celebrated on the full moon day (Pournami) of the Tamil month of Karthigai, The Arulmigu Arunachaleswarar Temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is one of the Pancha Bhoota Stalams, representing the element of fire (Agni). The temple also hosts a 10-day festival called Maha Deepam or Annamalai Deepam.

    For more info →

    Makaravilakku Sabarimala

    The Makara Jyothi, also known as the Makaravilakku, is a celestial event that happens annually at the Sabarimala Temple in Kerala on Makara Sankranti.

    It is a divine light that appears when the sun sets over the Ponnambalamedu hills, casting a visible light from the temple premises.

    For more info →

    Puri Rath Yatra

    This is the largest and oldest Hindu chariot festival, and is held in the city of Puri, Odisha.

    The festival features three massive wooden chariots carrying the deities Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra. The chariots are pulled by devotees on a grand avenue to the Gundicha Temple, where the deities stay for a week. The festival symbolizes Lord Jagannath's visit to his maternal aunt's home.

    For more info →

    Maha Kumbh Mela - "Festival of the sacred pitcher"

    The world's largestpeaceful congregation of pilgrims on earth, during which participants bathe or take a dip in a sacred river. It's held every 12 years at one of four sacred sites in India, and the location rotates between the sites:

    Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh: The Maha Kumbh Mela is held here, at the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna, and Saraswati rivers.

    Haridwar, Uttarakhand: Held on the banks of the Ganges.

    Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh: Held on the banks of the Shipra.

    Nashik, Maharashtra: Held on the banks of the Godavari.

    For more info →

    Arulmigu Subramaniya Swamy Temple in Tiruchendur & the Sri Subramanya Swamy Temple in Tiruttani

    Arulmigu Subramaniya Swamy Temple in Tiruchendur: This temple hosts a spectacular Kavadi festival that combines religion, sacrifice, tradition, and splendor.

    Sri Subramanya Swamy Temple in Tiruttani: This temple hosts the Aadi Krithigai festival, which includes a Kavadi festival. The festival is celebrated with a three-day float festival and attracts over 400,000 devotees. Devotees carry a Kavadi, which is a semicircular wooden log with offerings tied to both ends, and climb the 365 steps of a hillock at the temple.

    Maha Shivratri

    Observing a Fast (Vrat): Many devotees abstain from food and water (Nirjala Vrat) or consume only fruits and milk (Phalahari Vrat) to detoxify the body and calm the mind.

    Night Vigil (Jagaran):It is traditional to stay awake all night, often sitting with a straight spine to benefit from the planet's unique energy alignment during this time.

    Abhishekam & Puja:Worshipers bathe the Shivalinga with sacred offerings like water, milk, honey, and ghee. Each represents a different blessing, such as purity or strength.

    Chanting & Meditation: Devotees chant "Om Namah Shivaya" or perform the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra throughout the night to focus their devotion.

    Sri Rama Navami

    Sri Rama Navami is a major Hindu festival celebrating the birth of Lord Rama, the seventh avatar of Lord Vishnu. Falling on the ninth day of the bright half of the lunar month of Chaitra (March–April), it marks the culmination of the spring Chaitra Navaratri.

    Prasadam: Devotees observe a strict fast throughout the day, breaking it at sunset with special festive foods like Panakam (a sweet jaggery drink), and moong dall

    Temple Rituals: Temples are elaborately decorated, and idols of Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, and Hanuman are often carried in grand processions.

    Scriptural Recitation: Devotees read passages from the Ramayana and Rama Charitamanas, participate in bhajans (devotional songs), and listen to spiritual discourses.
