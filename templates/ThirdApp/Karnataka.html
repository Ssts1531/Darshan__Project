Virupaksha Temple

Virupaksha temple is a prominent religious centre in Hampi dedicated to Lord Shiva. Virupaksha is a form of Shiva where his third eye is open and regarded as the supreme lord who governs everything.

Shiva was the patron deity of the Vijayanagara empire and was built on the banks of river Tungabhadra. The temple is famous architectural and engineering aspects. Special days like Maha Shivaratri, Chariot festival, and Hampi Utsav are marked and celebrated with great grandeur.

Udupi Sri Krishna Temple

The devotees get a glimpse of the deity, Balakrishna from a window divided into nine squares known as Navagraha Kindi.

The eight mathas of the temple work together in carrying out its everyday functions and celebrate Udupi Paryaya Utsava, a bi-annual festival. It is amongst Karnataka’s most visited temples and a significant community centre.

Dharmasthala

‘Dharma’ means righteousness and ‘sthala’ means place. So, the shrine upholds justice and universal laws like honesty, integrity and equality. It is one of the finest examples of cultural amalgamation in a religious setting.

The administrative affairs of the temple are managed by a Jain family known as ‘Heggades’. The current administrator is Veerendra Heggade who is known for spearheading many social welfare programmes. Apart from this, the temple is in the lap of nature. It makes for an amazing ride on the winding roads of the Western Ghats.

Sringeri Sharada Peetha

Sringeri is one of the major temples frequently visited by locals. Goddess Saraswati is the main deity bestowing wisdom and intelligence.

On the banks of Tunga, it is a peaceful place amid the Malenadu hills. The temple is associated with the Advaita philosophy by Adi Shankaracharya. Advaita philosophy refers to the Upanishads and believes in the non-duality of self.

The exquisitely carved gopuram and temple walls of the Vidya Shankara temple are bewitching.

Kukke Subramanya Temple

Kukke Subramanya is a Hindu temple of Subramanya, the son of Lord Shiva and Parvati. The Bramhagiri hills backdrop the temple and the Kumaradhara river flows in its vicinity.

The temple is sacred as it performs various rituals to solve sarpa doshas.

Kukke Subramanya temple allows devotees from all castes and religions. It is a significant temple as Hindus believe it resolves all troubles in marriage, childbirth, education, and career.

Chamundeshwari Temple

The goddess Chamundeshwari, the patron deity of the Wodeyars defeated Mahishasura, a demon who tortured the people of Mysore. The goddess bestows courage and strength to overcome the evils in one's life.

The temple follows the Dravidian architecture style with massive and ornately carved gopura. A statue of Nandi, the vahana (vehicle) of Lord Shiva is in the Chamundi hills.

During the Dasara festival, goddess Chamundeshwari is carried in a golden howdah on the royal elephant.

Lord Nanjundeshwara or SriKanteshwara is worshipped in the temple. Both names signify the power of Lord Shiva. According to Hindu mythology.

Lord Shiva consumed poison and held it in his throat to save humanity. That turned his neck blue and is called Neelakant.

Sri Annapoorneshwari Temple Horanadu

Annaporneshwari means the ‘Goddesses of food’ and the deity bestows its devotees with good yield and prosperity. Accordingly, the temple offers free meals and snacks as prasadha to its devotees all year round.

Dodda Basavana Gudi / Bull Temple

Bull temple is one of the few historical temples in Bangalore. Kempegowda, the founder of the city, constructed the temple in the 16th century.

The main deity is Nandi or a sacred bull who is the vahana of Lord Shiva. Kadalekai Parishe or groundnut fair is held in the vicinity of the temple every year in November.

The freshly picked groundnuts are offered to Nandi as farmers seek blessings for good yield.

Jain centres in South India are called ‘Basadi’.
