Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Mandir

Ayodhya is a holy city situated on the banks of river Sarayu in Uttar Pradesh. It is one of the top places to visit in Uttar Pradesh, and among the most popular pilgrimage sites in India. It is about 135 km from Gorakhpur, and 140 km from Lucknow.

Ayodhya or Awadh is the birthplace of the Hindu deity, Lord Ram Shri Ram. This holy city has been regarded as the first one of the seven most important pilgrimage sites for Hindus, and among the prominent places to visit near Kanpur.

It is believed that a temple stood at the supposed birth spot of Rama, which was demolished by the orders of the Mughal emperor Babur and a mosque was erected in its place that was later demolished by Hindu mobs. The construction of the Ram Mandir commenced in 2020 and is the must-include in Ayodhya trip.

The temple is of great religious significance for the Hindu communities. As a tourist, the undeniable spiritual vibe around the temple, accentuated by the constant sound of hymns and the chants of 'Jai Shree Ram', will surely swoon your senses.

Kashi Vishwanath Temple

In the sacred city of Varanasi, Kashi Vishwanath Jyotirlinga is among the Dwadasa Jyotirlinga which is located close to the banks of the Ganga River and captures the attention of millions of pilgrims to offer their prayers and seek blessings from Lord Shiva.

The temple of Kashi Vishwanath is also known as the ‘Golden Temple of Varanasi’ because of its gold-plated dome.

The Kashi Vishwanath beautifully represents the cycle of life and death.

While wandering through the narrow lanes around the temple, visitors can hear the songs of the bells ringing, the view of the holy aartis and the feel of spirituality that will fill their hearts with wholesome divinity.

The Kashi Vishwanath Temple has been a source of spiritual inspiration for many saints and sages.

History and Spiritual Significance of Kashi Vishwanath Jyotirlinga Temple

The history of the Kashi Vishwanath Jyotirlinga temple dates back to around 2500 years ago as it is believed that the temple was built by King Vikramaditya.

The temple also occurs in the Kasha Kandra, an ancient document that the temple might be as old as the sixth century. The temple is even mentioned in several holy books of Hindus.

The legendary story that is associated with the Jyotirlinga is that Lord Shiva presented himself as a beam of a huge pillar to solve the problem between Lord Brahma and Lord Vishnu where they would need to find the ends of the pillar to become the supreme power.

No one succeeded in finding the ends of the pillar but Lord Brahma lied about the fact and made Shiva curse him to be not worshipped by anyone and gave the title of supremacy to Lord Vishnu.

After the huge pillar disappeared from the three realms, a small portion of it remained here and became the Vishwanath Jyotirlinga.

Temple Timings: 03:00 AM to 11:00 PM

How to Reach?

Nearest Airport: Lal Bahadur Shastri International Airport
Nearest Railway Station: Varanasi Junction

Special Attraction: People believe that while looking at the golden spire if one asks for a wish, it gets fulfilled.

Also, it is believed that if a person lives or dies here will attain moksha.

Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi Temple

At a distance of 56 km from Agra, 159 km from Delhi, 419 km from Lucknow and 224 km from Jaipur, Mathura is an ancient city and also the district headquarters of Mathura in the state of Uttar Pradesh. Mathura is one of the popular tourist places near Agra.

Mathura is one of the seven cities (Sapta Puri) considered holy by Hindus, the other six are Ayodhya, Haridwar, Varanasi, Kanchipuram, Ujjain and Dwarka.

It is located at the banks of river Yamuna and is popularly known as the Brajbhoomi, the sacred land of Lord Krishna.

The Sri Krishna Janmabhoomi temple is home to a prison cell called Garbha Griha that is believed to be the exact birthplace of Krishna. Large number of devotees visit this temple every year on Janmashtami.

The city of Mathura has a rich history. In the ancient times, Mathura was an important point of culture and civilization.

In the epic of Ramayana, the Ikshwaku prince Shatrughna slays a demon called Lavanasura and claims the land. Afterwards, the place came to be known as Madhuvan as it was thickly wooded, then Madhupura and later Mathura.

The best time to visit the temple is during the Janmashtami festival, as the entire city celebrates it with great fanfare.

Many rituals are done to celebrate the birth of the lord, and special celebrations take place within the temple premises that are worth seeing.

Banke Bihari Temple - UP

The image of Bihariji installed in the Shri Banke Bihari Mandir, is the one granted to Swami Haridas by the celestial couple Shyama-Shyam themselves. Submitting to the desire of devotees The Lord appeared in person with his divine consort and left back a black charming image before disappering.

Swami Haridas Ji was born to Shri Ashudhir and his wife Smt Gangadevi on the day of Radha Ashtami i.e. eighth day of second (bright) fortnight of Bhadrapad month of the year 1535 Vikrami(1478 A.D.). He was born in a small village, now known as Haridaspur, near Aligarh in Uttar Pradesh. The linage of the family can be traced back to Shri Gargacharya. Shri Gargachrya was the kulguru (family guru) of Yadavs and visited Brij secretly for conducting the Namkaran Samskar (naming ceremony) of young Krishna and Balram on request of Sri Vasudev. A branch of the family migrated to Multan (now in Pakistan), but some of them returned after a long time. Sri Ashudhir was one such migrant who after returning from Multan settled at the outskirts of Brij, near Aligarh.

Now that the Goswamis of the temple realized the need for feeding Lord during the hours of rest also, it was decided that a box containing four laddoos along with water and beeris (betel leaves) be kept on a side table during the night as well as afternoon when the temple is closed.

The online activities in the sewa of Thakur Shri Banke Bihariji Maharaj are being run independently of the temple management committee, which is primarily responsible for overseeing the daily affairs of the temple.

Prem Mandir

Prem Mandir, which literally translates to the "Temple of Divine Love," is a massive and spiritually significant Hindu temple complex located on the outskirts of the holy town of Vrindavan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh.

Conceived and established by the fifth original Jagadguru, Shri Kripalu Ji Maharaj, construction began in January 2001. It took 11 years and approximately ₹150 crore to complete before its grand public opening in February 2012. Today, it is maintained by the non-profit spiritual trust Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat.

Architecture: The two-story monument is crafted entirely out of premium white Italian Carrara marble. It stands 125 feet high on a 54-acre complex, meticulously sculpted by over 1,000 artisans using elements of classical Indian architecture.

Presiding Deities: The temple is unique in housing two separate sanctums. The ground floor is dedicated to Shri Radha Govind (Radha-Krishna), while the upper level houses Shri Sita-Ram.

The 20-foot-deep granite foundation combined with the usage of premium white Italian Carrara marble in the temple’s construction ensures that it will be able to withstand the test of time and benefit seekers for generations to come.

A circumambulation route has been constructed on the mandir prāṅgaṇ, the platform of the temple, enabling visitors to view the 84 panels depicting the loving pastimes of Shri Radha Krishna which are carved on the outer walls of the temple.

The Evening Glow & Fountain Show: A major tourist attraction is the temple’s dynamic lighting system. After sunset, the entire white marble structure dramatically changes colors every few minutes. Visitors can also view a daily musical light and sound fountain show that recreates historical spiritual stories.

Timings: 8:30 AM – 12:00 PM and 4:30 PM – 8:30 PM daily.

Musical Fountain: Usually takes place between 7:00 PM and 8:00 PM (varying slightly between winter and summer seasons).

Hanuman Garhi

The story of Hanuman Garhi spans millennia - from mythological beginnings to becoming Ayodhya's most beloved temple.

Mythological Origins -Ancient Era(Treta Yuga): According to Hindu mythology, Lord Hanuman chose this hilltop as his residence to guard Ayodhya and protect the birthplace of Lord Ram for eternity.

Temple Construction -10th Century(~900 CE): The current structure of Hanuman Garhi is believed to have been constructed around the 10th century CE, though the site was sacred long before that.

Nawabi Era & Resilience -Medieval Period(12th-16th Century): During the medieval period, the temple faced various challenges but continued to be a center of devotion. Local rulers and saints helped preserve its sanctity.

Cultural Significance Grows -Mughal Period(16th-18th Century): Despite political turbulence, Hanuman Garhi remained a symbol of faith. The Naga sadhus played a crucial role in protecting the temple during this period.

Recorded History -British Period(19th Century): British records document Hanuman Garhi as one of the most important temples in Ayodhya, visited by thousands of pilgrims annually.

Renovations & Growth -Modern Era(20th Century): Multiple restoration and beautification projects were undertaken. The temple became a mandatory stop for pilgrims visiting Ayodhya.

A Beacon of Faith -Present Day(21st Century): With the construction of Ram Mandir nearby, Hanuman Garhi has seen unprecedented footfall. It remains Ayodhya's most visited temple with over 42,000+ reviews.

The Sacred Hilltop Temple:

Shri Hanuman Garhi is one of the most prominent and revered temples in Ayodhya, the holy city in Uttar Pradesh, India. Perched atop a hillock in the heart of the city, this 10th-century temple is accessed by climbing 76 sacred steps - each step symbolizing a step closer to the divine.

The temple is built in the form of a four-sided fort (Garhi), giving it the distinctive name "Hanuman Garhi." Its fort-like structure is a testament to the belief that Lord Hanuman stands as the eternal guardian of Ayodhya.

Spiritual Significance:

According to mythology, this is the very place where Lord Hanuman lived in a cave to guard the city of Ayodhya. Devotees believe that before visiting Ram Janmabhoomi, one must first seek the blessings of Hanuman at Hanuman Garhi.

The main deity in the temple is Maa Anjani holding Bal Hanuman (child Hanuman) on her lap - a rare and deeply significant form of worship found in very few temples across India. This form represents the loving bond between a mother and her divine child.

The temple complex is believed to be the spot where Hanuman meditated and kept vigil over Ayodhya for centuries. The spiritual energy of this place is said to fill devotees with courage, devotion, and inner peace.

Architecture & Design:

The temple showcases a unique blend of medieval Indian architectural styles. Built like a fortress, it has circular bastions at each corner and thick walls that reflect its protective symbolism.

Inside the main sanctum, the idol of Maa Anjani with Bal Hanuman is adorned with sindoor (vermillion), flowers, and garlands. The cave-like inner chamber creates an intimate and profoundly spiritual atmosphere.

The temple courtyard offers a panoramic view of Ayodhya, making it not just a spiritual destination but also a vantage point to admire the ancient holy city.

Connection with Ram Janmabhoomi:

Hanuman Garhi holds a special place in the religious circuit of Ayodhya. The tradition says that no visit to Ayodhya is complete without first paying respects at Hanuman Garhi. It is believed that Hanuman ji himself grants permission to devotees to proceed to Ram Janmabhoomi.

This deep connection between Hanuman and Lord Ram is at the heart of Ayodhya's spiritual identity. Hanuman is considered the first devotee of Ram, and his presence in Ayodhya through this temple reaffirms the eternal bond between servant and lord.

Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple

Sankat Mochan Hanuman Mandir is located on the tranquil banks of the Assi River in Varanasi. This famous Hindu temple of India symbolizes faith and devotion and has a rich history dating back to the 16th century. The poet-saint Tulsidas established the Hindu temple, dedicating it to the Hindu God Hanuman.

Once inside, devotees can purchase “besan ke ladoo,” a special sweet, to present to Lord Hanuman as prasad, also known as an offering. Aside from the beautiful historical architecture, devotees will see the idol, Lord Hanuman, positioned to face Lord Rama, reflecting his unyielding and selfless worship of his divine Lord, adorned in stunning marigold flowers.

Goswami Tulisdas is widely regarded as a great thinker. He remains one of the most important figures in Indian culture, especially for his greatest masterpiece, Ramcharitmanas. According to many, Goswami Tulsidas found inspiration for Ramcharitmanas in the tranquil environment of the Mochan Hanuman temple grounds.

After Tulsidas' passing, other spiritual leaders continued his work. To this day, they work to further enrich the temple’s expansive heritage. The work they have already completed has formed the spiritual haven it is today.

Now, devotees can visit the temple for worship and experience the same peace that Tulsidas felt when he established it. With the availability of daily worship in the morning and evening, the Sankat Mochan Temple is open to people with all levels of devotion, whether they visit the temple twice per day or once per week.

Today, Prof. Vishwambhar Nath Mishra, Sankat Mochan Hanuman mandir’s current mahant, is continuing the legacy of his predecessor, the late Prof. Veer Bhadra Mishra. As a result of his leadership, the temple has continued to thrive as a place of worship, with teachings rich with deities like Ram, Shiva, Vishnu, and Krishna resonating within its walls, deepening the devotees' spiritual connections. Moreover, the temple.has also been a place of significant celebration tied to a vast array of Hindu traditions.
