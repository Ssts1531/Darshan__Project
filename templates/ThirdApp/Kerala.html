The main idol is massive and intricately crafted using Katu-Sarkara-Yogam, who is depicted reclining on the serpent Adishesha in the "Anantha Shayana" posture.

Requiring devotees to view the Lord through three separate doorways to see his head, midriff, & feet.

The iconic 7-tier, 100-foot Gopuram reflects a brilliant fusion of Kerala and Tamil architectural styles.

Sree Ayyappa Temple

Sabarimala Sree Dharma Sastha Temple, dedicated to Lord Ayyappa, is the most famous and prominent among all the Sastha temples in Kerala.

Devotees carry a sacred, twin-compartment cloth bag called Irumudi Kettu on their heads. It contains offerings, most notably ghee-filled coconuts.

The final ascent to the sanctum requires climbing 18 sacred steps. Traditionally, only pilgrims who have completed the Irumudi Kettu are permitted to climb these steps.

The bright celestial star (Sirius) that appears in the evening sky above the Ponnambalamedu hill during mid January.

The festivals are as follows: Guruvayur Utsavam, Ekadashi Festival, Chembai Music Festival, Mandala Pooja, Saraswathy Pooja, Thiruvonam, Ashtami Rohini, Nira Puthari, Vaishakha, Vishu, Kucheladinam, Samkrama.

Wadakkunathan Temple

According to legends the original “Prathishta” was done by Lord Parasurama, an incarnation of Vishnu and it must have been done at least four thousand years ago.

The main deity of the temple is Lord Siva, other idols installed in the temple are those to Sree Parvati, Ganesha, Sankaranarayana and Sree Rama. Parvati, is sanctify in the sanctum of Lord Shiva it self in opposite direction.

Sree Wadakkunathan Temple won 2015 UNESCO ASIA PACIFIC Heritage Award of Excellence.

Chottanikkara Temple

The Divine Mother known as Rajarajeswari (Adiparasakthi) is worshipped here in three forms Saraswathy in the morning, Lakshmi at noon and Durga in the evening.

here is an Idol of Mahavishnu on the same pedestal and so the Deity is called Ammenarayana, Devinarayana, Lakshminarayana and Bhadrenarayana also. Along with Lakshmi & Narayana there are idols of Brahma, Siva, Ganapathi (Ganesh), Subramanya and Sastha on the same pedestal.

"MAKOM Thozhal" is the most important festival of the temple which is celebrated in the month of Kumbham. (Feb / March)

Mannarasala Nagaraja Temple

The Mannarasala Sree Nagaraja Temple is the largest serpent worship temple in the state, housing over 30,000 snake images.

The most significant celebration occurs on the Ayilyam day of the Malayalam month of Thulam (October/November).

The most popular offering is the Uruli Kamazhthal—placing a brass or bronze vessel upside down before the deity to seek blessings for fertility.

Kodungallur Bhagavathy Temple

Sree Kurumba Bhagavathy Temple in Kerala is one of India's oldest functioning temples, famed as the head of 64 Bhadrakali shrines.

Five 'Sri Chakras', said to have been installed by Adi Shankaracharya, are believed to be the main source of the powers of this deity.

Bharani festival, Thalappoli festival, Navarathri festival, Chandattam are the major festivals.

Thirunelli Temple (Wayanad)

Thirunelli Temple is an ancient Maha Vishnu shrine nestled in the Brahmagiri Hills of Wayanad, Kerala.

It is renowned as the "Kashi of the South" and is the only temple in India where devotees can perform all ancestral and life-cycle rituals in one location.

In this holy land, the Papanasini flows as the source of salvation. Papanasini is born somewhere in Brahmagiri.

The nearby Gunnika cave temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva.

Aazhimala Shiva Temple

This Ancient place was one of the hide out of Pandavas who were in disguise. Natural caves are found still in between the rocks. To quench Panchalis thirst, Bheemasena had to make a hole in the rock with his knee to fill the water is a reminder of ancient times.

The Azhimala Shiva Temple in Kerala is most famous for its towering 58-foot-tall statue of Lord Shiva in the Gangadhareshwara form.

It hosts significant cultural and spiritual gatherings, most notably the Naranga Vilakku (held annually in January/February) and Maha Shivaratri.
